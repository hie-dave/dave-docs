Harvesting and vegetation removal

Harvesting in DAVE is an accounting event as well as a change to the trees in a stand. Cutting removes some or all of the represented trees and divides their carbon and nitrogen among material exported from the ecosystem, material left as litter, and, when enabled, a longer lived harvested-product pool. Exported carbon is reported as a harvest flux in the year of removal. The product pool releases its carbon later. These bookkeeping categories do not specify a real-world use for the wood.

What determines the fate of a cut tree?

Material Typical treatment
Stem wood A harvest-efficiency fraction is exported. Uncollected wood remains as woody litter.
Twigs and branches A specified residue fraction can be removed; the rest becomes litter. Some leaves attached to removed twigs leave with them.
Coarse roots and stumps A specified residue fraction can be removed; the rest becomes woody litter.
Leaves and fine roots Usually enter litter, except for leaves removed with twigs.
Exported stem wood If ifslowharvestpool is enabled, a PFT-specific share enters the harvested-product pool; the remainder is accounted for as a harvest carbon flux in that year.

The fractions apply to the part of a tree that is cut. A thinning can cut a fraction of the represented trees, whereas a clearcut normally removes all eligible trees in the patch. Nitrogen follows corresponding exported or litter paths. Stem, twig, and coarse-root shares depend on PFT traits, so equal harvest settings can produce different fluxes for different species.

For ordinary forest management, harv_eff_thin and harv_eff_cc control the stem harvest fraction during thinning and clearcutting. The matching res_outtake_twig_* and res_outtake_coarse_root_* settings control residue removal. If a management value is unset, stem efficiency and twig removal use PFT settings, while coarse-root removal defaults to 0.1. The first scheduled thinning in a clearcut rotation is precommercial: its cut biomass goes to litter instead of exported products. See Management types for the full parameter reference. A cut from land-cover conversion is also accounted for as harvest, but is reported with land-use-change fluxes rather than ordinary wood-harvest fluxes; see Land cover and land-use change.

Treeinit removal

At the end of its prescribed establishment year, treeinit removes the vegetation already present in affected patches before placing the prescribed cohorts. For existing trees, it cuts the whole represented cohort, exports all eligible stem wood and removable twig and coarse-root material, and removes fine roots. Some leaves still become litter. This event does not put removed material into the slow harvested-product pool, even when that pool is enabled for ordinary harvests. Existing grasses are removed through mortality accounting, which adds their biomass to litter rather than treating them as harvested wood.

Treeinit's removal settings are fixed by that operation: changing the management2 harvest-efficiency or residue settings changes later cuts, not this initial replacement. The added trees and their prescribed carbon and nitrogen are explained in Prescribed tree establishment.

Reading outputs

Interpret a decrease in live biomass together with litter, harvested-product, and harvest-flux outputs. A cut can transfer substantial carbon into litter without exporting it; decomposition then affects later carbon fluxes. The product pool can defer a portion of the export-associated carbon flux. If treeinit is combined with forest_init_poolsoil 1, soil and litter pools from the affected stands are pooled before the replacement, so compare the stand and grid-cell totals as well as individual patch values.
