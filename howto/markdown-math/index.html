Writing Equations in Markdown

This page is a practical reference for writing mathematical equations on this documentation site. If you are familiar with Word's equation editor but new to LaTeX, start here.

Equations are written in LaTeX syntax, which is the standard for typesetting mathematics in scientific publishing. The site renders them automatically wherever you write them.

Inline vs Display Equations

There are two ways to include an equation:

Type Syntax When to use
Inline $...$ Part of a sentence, e.g. "where \(x\) is…"
Display $$...$$ Standalone, centred equation on its own line

Inline example — the source:

The leaf area index $L$ is the one-sided area of leaf tissue per unit
ground area (m² m⁻²).

renders as:

The leaf area index \(L\) is the one-sided area of leaf tissue per unit ground area (m² m⁻²).

Display example — the source:

$$
E = mc^2
$$

renders as:

\[ E = mc^2 \]

Tip

Use display equations for anything that has multiple terms, fractions, or needs to be easy to refer to. Use inline for short symbols and simple expressions that flow naturally in a sentence.

Subscripts and Superscripts

What Syntax Result
Subscript x_i \(x_i\)
Superscript x^2 \(x^2\)
Both x_i^2 \(x_i^2\)
Multi-character subscript x_{ij} \(x_{ij}\)
Multi-character superscript x^{n+1} \(x^{n+1}\)

Use curly braces {} whenever the subscript or superscript is more than one character.

$$
A_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} B_{ik} \, C_{kj}
$$
\[ A_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} B_{ik} \, C_{kj} \]

Fractions

$\frac{numerator}{denominator}$

Examples:

Syntax Result
\frac{a}{b} \(\frac{a}{b}\)
\frac{dA}{dt} \(\frac{dA}{dt}\)
\frac{1}{2} \(\frac{1}{2}\)

In display math, fractions are larger and easier to read:

$$
R_n = \frac{G_s \, I_{dir}}{1 + k_x \cdot L}
$$
\[ R_n = \frac{G_s \, I_{dir}}{1 + k_x \cdot L} \]

Square Roots and nth Roots

$\sqrt{x}$          % square root
$\sqrt[n]{x}$       % nth root
Syntax Result
\sqrt{2} \(\sqrt{2}\)
\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \(\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\)
\sqrt[3]{x} \(\sqrt[3]{x}\)

Sums, Products, and Integrals

These are written with limits using _ (lower) and ^ (upper):

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i
$$

$$
\prod_{i=1}^{n} x_i
$$

$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} f(x) \, dx
$$
\[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \qquad \prod_{i=1}^{n} x_i \qquad \int_{0}^{\infty} f(x) \, dx \]

For double integrals: \iint, triple: \iiint, and surface/contour: \oint.

Use \ before function names so they are typeset upright (not italic):

\ln x \(\ln x\) \log x \(\log x\)
\log_{10} x \(\log_{10} x\) \max \(\max\)
\min \(\min\) \lim_{x \to 0} \(\lim_{x \to 0}\)

Spacing

LaTeX removes whitespace inside math mode. Use these commands to add space:

Command Space
\, thin space (most common)
\; medium space
\quad large space
\qquad double large space 
$f(x) \, dx$       % thin space before dx — conventional
$A \quad B$        % gap between terms

Brackets and Delimiters

Plain brackets ( ) and [ ] work directly. For tall expressions, use \left and \right to scale them automatically:

$$
\left( \frac{a}{b} \right)^2
$$
\[ \left( \frac{a}{b} \right)^2 \]

Other delimiters: \left[ \right], \left\{ \right\}, \left| \right| (absolute value), \left\| \right\| (norm).

Multi-line Equations with align

To align a derivation or a system of equations at the = sign, use the align environment. Use & to mark the alignment point and \\ to end each line:

$$
\begin{align}
A &= \pi r^2 \\
C &= 2 \pi r
\end{align}
$$
\[ \begin{align} A &= \pi r^2 \\ C &= 2 \pi r \end{align} \]

A multi-step derivation:

$$
\begin{align}
E_a &= E_{a,0} \cdot f(T) \\
    &= E_{a,0} \cdot \exp\!\left( \frac{T - T_0}{10} \right)
\end{align}
$$
\[ \begin{align} E_a &= E_{a,0} \cdot f(T) \\ &= E_{a,0} \cdot \exp\!\left( \frac{T - T_0}{10} \right) \end{align} \]

Equation Numbering

Display equations in the equation environment are numbered automatically:

$$
\begin{equation}
GPP = A_{canopy} \cdot \Delta t
\end{equation}
$$

To add a manual tag to any display equation, use \tag{}:

$$
NPP = GPP - R_a \tag{1}
$$
\[ NPP = GPP - R_a \tag{1} \]

Matrices

Use the pmatrix (parentheses), bmatrix (square brackets), or matrix (no brackets) environments:

$$
\mathbf{A} =
\begin{pmatrix}
a_{11} & a_{12} \\
a_{21} & a_{22}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
\[ \mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} \end{pmatrix} \]

Bold upright symbols for vectors and matrices: \mathbf{A} → \(\mathbf{A}\), \mathbf{v} → \(\mathbf{v}\).

Text Inside Equations

Use \text{...} to include upright text inside an equation (units, conditions, labels):

$$
R_s \quad [\text{W m}^{-2}]
$$

$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } x > 0 \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$
\[ f(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } x > 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]
